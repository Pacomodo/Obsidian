___

**DEF**) An additive subgroup $N$ of a [[Ring|ring]] $R$ satisfying the properties $$aN \subseteq N, Nb \subseteq N \ \ \ \ \forall a,b \in R $$is an **ideal**.

___

**EXAMPLE**)

* $n\mathbb{Z}$ is an ideal in the ring $\mathbb{Z}$.
It is obvious that $n\mathbb{Z}$ is a subring of $\mathbb{Z}$. and for all $s \in \mathbb{Z}$, $s(nm) = (nm)s = n(ms) \in n\mathbb{Z}$.

* Let $F =$ The ring of all functions $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$. Let $C =$ The subring of $F$ consisting of all the constant functions in $F$.
$C$ is not an ideal in $F$. Consider the product of $\mathrm{sin} x$ and $2$. It is $2 \mathrm{sin}x \notin C$.

* Let $F$ is same and $N \leq F$ such that $f(2) = 0$.
$N$ is an ideal in $F$.
Let $f \in N, g \in F$. Then $(fg)(2) = f(2)g(2) = 0g(2) = 0$, so $fg \in N$. Similarly, $gf \in N$.
Just observing that $N$ is the [[대학 수업/대수학/책 정리/Section 13/Definition/Kernel|kernel]] of the [[Evaluation homomorphism|evaluation homomorphism]] $\phi _2 : F \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$.
