- We noted that the individual distributions of two r.v.s do not tell us anything about whether the r.v.s are independent or dependent.
- For example, two $\text{Bern}(1/2)$ r.v.s $X$ and $Y$ could be independent if they indicate Heads on two diﬀerent coin ﬂips, or dependent if they indicate Heads and Tails, respectively, on the same coin ﬂip.
- However, we usually care about relationship between multiple r.v.s. in the same experiment.
- This chapter considers joint distributions, also called multivariate distributions, which capture the previously missing information about how multiple r.v.s interact.
