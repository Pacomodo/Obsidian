**THEOREM**)
Let $E$ be an [[Extension field]] of $F$. Let $\alpha \in E$, where $\alpha$ is [[Algebraic, transcendental|algebraic]] over $F$.

1. Then, there is an irreducible polynomial $p(x) \in F[x]$ such that $p(\alpha) = 0$.
2. This irreducible polynomial $p(x)$ is **UNIQUELY** determined up to a constant factor in $F$.
3. $p(x)$ is a polynomial of **MINIMAL** degree $\geq 1$ in $F[x]$ having $\alpha$ as a zero.
4. If $f(\alpha) = 0$ for $f(x) \in F[x]$ with $f(x) \neq 0$, then $p(x) | f(x)$.

___

**PROOF**)

(Class Handouts manner)

1. There is an irreducible polynomial $p(x) \in F[x]$ such that $p(\alpha) = 0$.

Let $\phi_\alpha$ be the [[Evaluation homomorphism]] of $F[x]$ into $E$ given by [[Thm 22.4]].
$$\phi_\alpha : F[x] \rightarrow E \ , \ f(x) \mapsto f(\alpha)$$
$Ker(\phi_\alpha)$ is an [[Ideal|ideal]].
By [[Thm 26.17|the fundemental homomorphism theorem]], $F[x] / Ker(\phi_\alpha) \cong \phi_\alpha(F[x]) \subset E$.
We know that $E$ is integral domain(moreover, $E$ is extension **field**) by [[Thm 19.9]], so, the image $\phi_\alpha(F[x])$ of ring homomorphism $\phi_\alpha$ is commutative ring with no [[Divisors of 0|zero divisors]], so, [[Integral Domain]].
So, $Ker(\phi_\alpha)$ is a [[Prime ideal|prime ideal]] by [[Thm 27.15]].
We know that $Ker(\phi_\alpha)$ is nonzero since $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$.([[Thm 29.12]])
Therefore $Ker(\phi_\alpha) = \left< p(x) \right>$ for some irreducible polynomial $p(x) \in F[x]$.
Now, (2)~(4) comes immediately from (1).

(Proof in Book)

Let $\phi_\alpha$ be the [[Evaluation homomorphism]] of $F[x]$ into $E$ given by [[Thm 22.4]].
$$\phi_\alpha : F[x] \rightarrow E \ , \ f(x) \mapsto f(\alpha)$$
$Ker(\phi_\alpha)$ is an [[Ideal|ideal]].
By [[Thm 27.24]], $Ker(\phi_\alpha)$ must be [[Principal ideal|principal]] generated by some $p(x) \in F[x]$. i.e. $Ker(\phi_\alpha) = \left< p(x) \right>$.
Thus, $f(\alpha) = 0 \Leftrightarrow f(x) \in Ker(\phi_\alpha) \Leftrightarrow f(x) \in \left< p(x) \right>$.
So, $p(x)|f(x)$.
Thus $p(x)$ is a polynomial of minimal degree $\geq 1$ having $\alpha$ as a zero.
Any other such polynomial of the same degree as $p(x)$ must be of the form $(a)p(x)$ for some $a \in F$.
So, it only remains for us to show that $p(x)$ is irreducible.
Suppose not. If $p(x) = r(x)s(x)$ is factorization of $p(x)$ into polynomials of lower degree, then, $p(\alpha)=0 \implies r(\alpha)s(\alpha)=0$.
Since $E$ is field, so, there is no zero divisor. Hence, either $r(\alpha) = 0$ or $s(\alpha) = 0$.
It contradicts that $p(x)$ is of minimal degree $\geq 1$ such that $p(\alpha) = 0$.
Thus, $p(x)$ is irreducible.
