**THEOREM**)
Let simple $G = (V, E)$ is given. $|V| = n. |E| = m$.
$A_G$ is adjacency matrix. $B_G$ is incidence matrix.
Then, $B_G B_G^T = A_{L(G)} + 2I_{|E|}$.

___

**PROOF**)
Consider $(B_G B_G^T)_{ij}$.
Note that $(B_G B_G^T)_{ij}$ $=$ the dot product of ($i$th row of $B_G$)&($j$th row of $B_G$) and ($i$th row of $B_G$) has the form of $(0, \cdots ,  1, \cdots , 1 , \cdots, 0)$ where index of $1$ value is $a$ and $b$ such that $e_i$ incident with $v_a$ and $v_b$.
if $i = j$, this value is $2$.
if $i \neq j$ and $e_i$ and $e_j$ share the same vertex, this value is $1$.
otherwise, this value is $0$.

Consider $A_{L(G)}$.
if $v$ is the eigenvector of $A_{L(G)}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$, then, $A_{L(G)}v = \lambda v = (B_GB_G ^T)v - 2v$
So, $(\lambda + 2) v = (B_GB_G ^T)v$.
This shows $v$ is eigenvector of $B_GB_G ^T$.
